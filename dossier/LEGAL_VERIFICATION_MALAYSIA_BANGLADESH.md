## 2. Malaysian Legal Framework: Statutes, Concessions, & Direct Employer Rights

### 2.1 Employment Act 1955 (Act 265) — Section 60K (Approval to Employ Foreign Employees)
* **Statutory Requirement:** Under Section 60K(1), no employer shall employ a foreign employee unless prior approval has been obtained from the Director General of Labour (Jabatan Tenaga Kerja Semenanjung Malaysia - JTKSM / KESUMA).
* **Direct Employer Standing:** The approval under Section 60K is granted **directly to the principal corporate employer** (the factory, plantation, or construction contractor). The law does *not* require the employer to hire an offshore intermediary syndicate.
* **Employer's Direct Liability:** Section 60K(3) mandates that the employer must furnish regular returns of foreign employees to the Director General.

### 2.2 Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 (Act 246 - Akta Agensi Pekerjaan Swasta)
* **Licensing Categories:** Act 246 governs employment agencies in Malaysia:
  * *Category A:* Local placement only.
  * *Category B:* Local placement and deploying Malaysians overseas.
  * *Category C:* Recruiting and placing foreign non-citizen workers into Malaysia.
* **Direct Hiring Exemption:** An employer recruiting foreign workers directly for their own operational workforce is **NOT** operating as an employment agency under Act 246 and does not require an APS license. The employer acts as the **Direct Principal**.
* **Prohibition of Unlawful Fees (Section 14 & 16):** Act 246 strictly prohibits charging unauthorized recruitment fees to workers.

### 2.3 Immigration Act 1959/63 (Act 155) & Immigration Regulations 1963
* **Regulation 11 — Pas Lawatan Kerja Sementara (PLKS) & Visa Dengan Rujukan (VDR):**
  * The issuance of a Calling Visa (VDR) is the sovereign prerogative of the **Director General of Immigration Malaysia (JIM)** under the Ministry of Home Affairs (KDN).
  * The employer applies for the VDR on behalf of named foreign candidates who have passed bio-medical screening.
* **Legal Status of FWCMS:**
  * **FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralized Management System)** is an enterprise IT concession contracted to **Bestinet Sdn Bhd**. FWCMS is an *administrative processing software*, not a statutory court or regulatory authority.
  * Bestinet has **zero statutory authority** to dictate which recruiting agencies in Bangladesh may deploy workers, nor does Bestinet have legal authority to impose private commercial taxes ($1,000 tolls) on foreign sovereign citizens without Cabinet and Parliamentary gazetting.
