### Node 1: Local Village Dalal / Union Broker
* **Profile**: Village-level informal intermediary, local political cadre, union parishad member relative, or returned ex-migrant.
* **Extraction per Worker**: **BDT 40,000 – BDT 60,000** (7.4% – 11.1% of total cost).
* **Mechanisms of Coercion & Sourcing**:
  1. **Information Asymmetry & Wage Hyper-Inflation**: Deceives rural youth by promising monthly wages of RM 3,000 – RM 4,000 (BDT 78,000 – 104,000), when the statutory Malaysian minimum wage is RM 1,500 (BDT 39,000) for a standard 8-hour workday.
  2. **Physical Passport Withholding**: Collects the worker's newly issued passport alongside an initial unreceipted cash "advance" or "registration deposit" of BDT 30,000 – BDT 50,000. Physical possession of the passport prevents the worker from seeking alternative legal migration pathways or negotiating price.
  3. **Collusion with Predatory Moneylenders (*Mohajons*)**: Brokers high-interest micro-loans for the candidate family through village moneylenders or local non-institutional lenders charging usurious rates of **5% to 10% per month** (compounded APR of 60% – 120%), securing the loan against homestead land deeds (*Dalil*) or gold collateral.

### Node 2: District Sub-Agent / Unlicensed Intermediary
* **Profile**: Operating in district towns (e.g., Tangail, Cumilla, Brahmanbaria, Noakhali, Bogura) out of unregistered commercial rooms, travel agencies, or coaching centers.
* **Extraction per Worker**: **BDT 60,000 – BDT 80,000** (11.1% – 14.8% of total cost).
* **Mechanisms of Consolidation & Logistics**:
  1. **Consolidation & Aggregation**: Batches 50 to 200 candidates from 10–20 village dalals. Acts as the primary regional credit aggregator.
  2. **Unregulated Medical Pre-Screening Extortion**: Directs workers to unaccredited private diagnostic centers for "pre-medical testing" (charging BDT 8,000 – 15,000 for standard blood/X-ray tests that cost BDT 2,000) to collect kickbacks from local clinics before sending them to official GAMCA/GCC centers.
  3. **Transit Staging & Forgery**: Houses candidates in substandard mess facilities in Dhaka (Kakrail, Naya Paltan, Arambagh) while forging technical training certificates (welding, pipefitting, construction safety) or biometrics verification paperwork without providing training.

### Node 3: Dhaka Syndicate Agent (102 BAIRA Quota Monopolists)
* **Profile**: Core syndicate of 100/101/102 recruiting agencies granted exclusive digital authorization within the Malaysian FWCMS system to process Bangladeshi labor demand, out of ~1,500 licensed BAIRA members.
* **Extraction per Worker**: **BDT 150,000 – BDT 220,000** (27.8% – 40.7% of total cost).
* **Mechanisms of Quota Capture & Laundering**:
  1. **Quota "Slot" Resale Market**: Because non-syndicate licensed agencies (~1,400 agencies) are blocked on the Malaysian digital portal, syndicate members sell processing "slots" or "pass-through numbers" to non-syndicate agencies and district brokers for **BDT 150,000 to BDT 200,000 in cash per candidate**.
  2. **Political War Chest Contributions & Bureaucratic Kickbacks**: Part of the extracted fee funds political kickbacks, payments to corrupt ministry officials, and institutional lobbying to maintain the closed syndicate model and suppress calls for an open market.
  3. **Cross-Border Hundi / Hawala Transfer**: Collects the full BDT 500k+ in cash from the candidate/sub-agent in Dhaka, retains their domestic margin (~BDT 60k-80k), and converts **RM 6,000 to RM 9,000 per worker (BDT 150,000 – BDT 230,000)** into foreign currency via Dhaka–Kuala Lumpur Hundi networks to pay Malaysian brokers (Node 4) and employers (Node 5).

### Node 4: Malaysian Cartel Broker / Intermediary Enterprise
* **Profile**: Malaysian shadow consultancy firms, human resource outsourcing companies, and politically linked fixers in Kuala Lumpur/Putrajaya.
* **Extraction per Worker**: **BDT 120,000 – BDT 180,000 (RM 4,600 – RM 6,900)** (22.2% – 33.3% of total cost).
* **Mechanisms of Regulatory & Digital Arbitrage**:
  1. **FWCMS Quota Hoarding & Manipulation**: Leverages connections with Malaysian regulatory bodies (Ministry of Home Affairs - KDN, and Ministry of Human Resources - KESUMA/KSM) to secure approved worker quotas in bulk under the Foreign Workers Centralized Management System.
  2. **Phantom Quota Creation**: Registers shell corporations or artificially inflates worker requirements for cleaning, manufacturing, and agricultural projects to acquire hundreds of approved calling visa quotas without real jobs.
  3. **Demand Letter Auctioning**: Sells these approved quota allocations to the Dhaka syndicate ringleaders at high spot prices (RM 4,000 to RM 8,000 per quota).

---

### Node 5: Malaysian Employer / Enterprise HR Manager
* **Profile**: HR Directors, procurement officers, plantation managers, and factory owners in Malaysian manufacturing, construction, plantation, and service sectors.
* **Extraction per Worker**: **BDT 60,000 – BDT 90,000 (RM 2,300 – RM 3,500)** (11.1% – 16.7% of total cost).
* **Mechanisms of Exploitation & EPP Inversion**:
  1. **Demand Letter Under-the-Table Auction**: Corrupt HR managers demand under-the-table cash kickbacks of **RM 1,500 to RM 3,500 per head** from recruiting brokers before signing official demand letters or attesting manpower requisitions.
  2. **Complete Inversion of Employer Pays Principle (EPP)**: Under Malaysian Law (Employment Act 1955) and ILO Convention 181, the employer is legally obligated to bear statutory immigration levies, round-trip airfare, medical screening, and processing charges. Instead, Malaysian employers pay zero recruitment costs, receive illicit cash kickbacks, and shift 100% of statutory fees and syndicate profit margins onto the Bangladeshi migrant.
  3. **Post-Arrival Wage Garnishment & Passport Confiscation**: Upon arrival in Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA), employers or outsourcing agents seize workers' passports, house them in crowded dormitories violating Workers' Minimum Standards of Housing and Amenities Act 1990 (Act 446), and illegally deduct levy costs (RM 150–200/month) from their basic wages.

## 5. ARCHITECTURAL VULNERABILITIES & SPECIFICATION FOR THE OPEN DIGITAL CLEARINGHOUSE

### 5.1 Root Architectural Flaws in the Current Regime
1. **Opaque Point-to-Point System Gatekeeping**: The bilateral digital architecture (FWCMS/Bestinet) was configured as a closed, proprietary whitelist rather than an open public API. This created an artificial monopoly where only 102 entities could transact.
2. **Complete Absence of Digital Audit Trail for Financial Flows**: While visa and medical data were digitized, **100% of financial transactions between workers, dalals, and agencies were conducted in physical cash or informal Hundi**, creating total deniability for syndicates.
3. **Inversion of Employer Responsibility**: Lack of strict bilateral verification enabled Malaysian employers to demand kickbacks rather than paying statutory recruitment costs, violating ILO Convention 181.
4. **Disconnection of Formal Banking (PKB) from Recruitment Gateways**: Formal banking institutions were completely bypassed because agencies refused to issue invoices reflecting actual market charges.
